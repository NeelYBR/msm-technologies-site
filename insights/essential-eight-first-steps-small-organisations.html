The Essential Eight for small organisations: where to start

You don't need a security team to make real progress. Here's the order we tackle the Essential Eight in for schools, firms and charities.

Priya Shah (DEMO author) · 20 Oct 2026 · 7 min read

Key points

  • MFA, patching, admin rights and tested backups stop most everyday attacks.
  • Reach Maturity Level 1 across all eight before going deeper.
  • Write down what you've done; insurers and boards will ask.

The Essential Eight is the Australian Cyber Security Centre's list of eight strategies that stop most everyday cyber attacks. It can look daunting on paper. In practice, a small organisation can make a big difference in a few weeks if it tackles things in the right order.

1. Turn on multi-factor authentication everywhere

Most break-ins we see start with a stolen password. MFA on email, remote access and admin accounts stops the majority of them. Start with leadership and finance staff, then roll it out to everyone.

2. Patch the things attackers actually target

Browsers, Microsoft 365, PDF readers and anything facing the internet (firewalls, VPNs) should be updated within days, not months. Automate it so nobody has to remember.

3. Take away everyday admin rights

Staff shouldn't log in as administrators to read email. Separate admin accounts, used only when needed, limit the damage from a single bad click.

4. Back up, and prove you can restore

Backups should be automatic, kept off-site and protected from deletion. Then actually test a restore. An untested backup is a hope, not a plan.

Tip: Quick win: check whether your Microsoft 365 data is backed up separately. Many organisations assume Microsoft does it for them.

Then build up the rest

  • Application control, so only approved software can run
  • Restricting Office macros
  • Hardening applications such as browsers and PDF readers
  • Regular reviews of who has access to what

Aim for Maturity Level 1 across all eight first, then lift the controls that matter most to your risk. Write down what you've done: insurers, auditors and boards will ask.

A realistic 90-day plan

  • Weeks 1–2: MFA for every account, starting with leadership, finance and admins
  • Weeks 3–4: automatic patching for operating systems, browsers and Microsoft 365 apps
  • Weeks 5–6: separate admin accounts and remove local admin from everyday users
  • Weeks 7–8: independent backups of servers and Microsoft 365, plus a test restore
  • Weeks 9–12: macro settings, application hardening, and a written summary for the board

Common traps we see

  • MFA by text message only, when an authenticator app is available
  • "Set and forget" patching that quietly stopped working months ago
  • Backups stored on the same network the attacker can reach
  • One shared admin password known by several staff and former contractors
Tip: If you only do one thing this week, check that every staff email account has MFA switched on. It takes minutes to check.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Essential Eight compulsory for us?

For most private organisations it's not a legal requirement, but it's the baseline insurers, funders and government customers increasingly expect. Schools and not-for-profits are often asked about it in grant or funding applications.

How long does Maturity Level 1 take?

For a typical organisation of 20 to 100 people, most of Level 1 can be in place within about three months, depending on how old the existing systems are.

Do we need new software?

Often not. Microsoft 365 Business Premium and modern Mac or Windows management already include much of what's needed. The work is in setting it up properly.
