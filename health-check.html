Free IT health check

How cyber-safe is your organisation?

15 quick questions based on the Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight and everyday IT basics. Your score updates as you go, and it takes about 3 minutes.

0/15 answered0%
  1. 1. Multi-factor authenticationEssential EightDoes every staff account need a second step (an app prompt or code) to sign in to email, Microsoft 365 or Google, and remote access?

    Stolen passwords are the most common way in. A second step stops most of these attacks cold.

  2. 2. Backups you can restoreEssential EightAre your important files and systems backed up automatically, with a copy kept offline or locked from changes, and has a restore been tested in the last 6 months?

    Ransomware often deletes or encrypts backups first. An untested backup is a hope, not a plan.

  3. 3. Microsoft 365 / Google backupBasicsIs your Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace data (email, OneDrive, SharePoint, Drive) backed up by a separate service?

    Cloud providers keep the service running, but deleted or encrypted data is usually your problem to recover.

  4. 4. Patching applicationsEssential EightAre browsers, Office, PDF readers and other apps updated automatically, with urgent security fixes applied within 48 hours?

    Attackers move quickly once a flaw is public. Out-of-date apps are an easy target.

  5. 5. Patching operating systemsEssential EightAre Windows, macOS, iPadOS and server updates installed within two weeks, and are all devices on a version that still gets security updates?

    Unsupported systems no longer get fixes, so every new flaw stays open forever.

  6. 6. Admin rightsEssential EightDo staff use everyday accounts without admin rights, with admin access kept to a few separate accounts used only for admin work?

    If a user with admin rights clicks a bad link, the attacker gets the keys to the whole computer, or worse.

  7. 7. Application controlEssential EightAre computers set up so that only approved software can run, rather than anything a user downloads?

    Blocking unknown programs stops a lot of malware from ever starting.

  8. 8. Office macrosEssential EightAre Microsoft Office macros blocked for files that come from the internet or email?

    Booby-trapped Word and Excel files are a classic way to sneak malware in.

  9. 9. Browser & app hardeningEssential EightAre web browsers and Office locked down centrally, for example blocking risky add-ons, old plugins and web ads that run code?

    Hardened browsers and apps leave fewer doors open for attackers.

  10. 10. Phishing awarenessBasicsDo staff get short security awareness training at least once a year, including how to spot and report a phishing email?

    People are your last line of defence. A quick report from a staff member can stop an attack early.

  11. 11. Device managementBasicsAre all laptops, tablets and phones that access work data enrolled in device management (such as Intune or Jamf), encrypted, and able to be wiped if lost?

    A lost laptop should be an inconvenience, not a data breach.

  12. 12. Antivirus & monitoringBasicsDo all computers and servers run managed antivirus or EDR that someone actually monitors and responds to?

    Alerts only help if someone sees them and acts, ideally within minutes.

  13. 13. Firewall & Wi-FiBasicsIs your firewall supported and up to date, with guest and student Wi-Fi kept separate from staff systems?

    Flat networks let one infected device reach everything else.

  14. 14. Passwords & leaversBasicsDo staff use a password manager, and are accounts for people who leave switched off on their last day?

    Reused passwords and forgotten accounts are easy wins for attackers.

  15. 15. Incident planBasicsDo you have a written plan for a cyber incident: who to call, how to keep working, and when to notify the OAIC or ACSC?

    In a crisis, a one-page plan saves hours of panic and helps you meet your notification obligations.

About this check

The Essential Eight is a set of mitigation strategies published by the Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC). This quiz is a quick, plain-English self-assessment, not a formal maturity assessment or audit. For a proper review of your systems, talk to our cyber team.
